How a no-cost EMI works

The seller sets the EMI at the price divided by the number of months, so the instalments add up to the price. The bank lends you less than the price: only the amount that those instalments repay at its interest rate. The gap is the interest, and the seller gives it to you as an upfront discount.

That discount covers the interest only. Two charges remain:

  • GST on the interest. The bank charges 18% GST on the interest each month, and it is billed to you.
  • The processing fee, and GST on the fee. You pay this once, at the start.

Worked example

A ₹60,000 purchase on a 6 month no-cost EMI, with the bank charging 16% a year and a ₹199 fee:

Monthly EMI (₹60,000 ÷ 6)₹10,000.00
Amount the bank really lends₹57,296.65
Seller discount (the interest)₹2,703.35
GST on the interest₹486.60
Processing fee plus GST₹234.82
Hidden cost₹721.44
Real rate, a year4.13%

The calculator rounds paise month by month, so the lines can differ from the total by a few paise.

So the plan costs about ₹721 more than the price, not nothing. The same ₹60,000 as a standard card EMI at 16% would cost ₹3,575 extra. The no-cost plan is far cheaper, but it is not free.

How the calculator works

  1. EMI = price ÷ months.
  2. Bank lends = the present value of those EMIs at your rate (the standard reducing-balance formula).
  3. Seller discount = price − bank lends.
  4. Each month: interest = balance × monthly rate, and GST = 18% of that interest.
  5. Hidden cost = everything you pay (EMIs, GST, fee and fee GST) − the price.
  6. Real rate = the yearly rate at which the price, less the day-one fee, grows into what you pay.

Questions

Is a no-cost EMI really free?
No. The bank still charges interest. The seller covers it with an upfront discount, so the instalments add up to the price. You still pay 18% GST on that interest and the processing fee with its own GST.
Who pays the GST on a no-cost EMI?
You do. The seller's discount covers the interest, but the GST charged on that interest is billed to your card each month.
Should I take a no-cost EMI or a cash discount?
Compare the discount you give up with the hidden cost shown above, and with what the money would earn if you paid in instalments. This page gives you the first number. The other two are your call.
Why is the real rate so low?
Because the seller covers the interest. The rate shown is only what is left: the GST on the interest and the fee, spread over what you effectively borrowed.
